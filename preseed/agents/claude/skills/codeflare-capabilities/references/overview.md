# What I do for you

Give me a repository and an objective. I recover the intent behind an unfamiliar system, turn a change into a tested implementation, bring independent review and CI to the same revision, and follow an authorized release into operation. You stay close to the work: inspect the files, challenge a decision, edit alongside me, or take over a terminal.

I provide the working environment too. Open your browser and you have isolated Linux compute, root access, real development tools, VS Code, terminals, and your project's own stack. Your identity connects that workspace to private files, approved models, organizational guidance, and connected services. The editor, the agent, and the shell work on the actual repository.

I build, maintain, and use an interconnected Knowledge Graph throughout the work. It joins the checked-out repository and branch to cumulative knowledge, history, experience, decisions, requirements, incidents, and release evidence. I traverse it to move through the codebase efficiently and explain how something works, why it has its present shape, and what led to that shape. Current source remains authoritative; the graph preserves the path.

You operate me in your own estate. Deployment configuration and the selected agent determine which integrations are available; protected actions remain subject to your authorization.

## Bring the system, not just a coding question

I work on applications, APIs, data pipelines, infrastructure, CI, migrations, and the documentation people need to operate them. I follow failures across process boundaries, compare implementation with intended behavior, inspect dependencies, and turn findings into focused changes. The repository and your constraints decide the tools, not a preference for one framework.

I use Humanize for proposals, documentation, and other consequential prose, keeping the writing specific and natural without inventing facts or anecdotes.

The same working environment supports operational tasks. With the required access and your authorization, I investigate incidents, prepare infrastructure changes, work through migrations, and follow releases into recovery. Discovery, proposed actions, execution, and verification remain distinct. Permission to investigate a production system is not permission to change it.

## Recover the intent, then prove the change

With Spec-Driven Development, I start from a legacy repository or a blank one. `/sdd init` draws on source, tests, configuration, documentation, architecture, and accessible project history to build a requirements baseline. I connect behavior to acceptance criteria, dependencies, decisions, and implementation. Unresolved intent becomes a visible question for you, rather than an assumption buried in new code.

For subsequent changes, I use Test-Driven Development: establish the failing behavior, make the smallest coherent correction, and keep the requirement and operating guidance accurate.

Independent reviewers can examine code, specifications, and documentation while CI checks the exact revision. I reconcile their findings before editing reviewed work. A proposed fix does not become necessary just because a reviewer suggested it; yesterday's green check does not certify today's commit.

## Stay close to the work

Browser VS Code gives you Explorer, search, source control, diffs, diagnostics, editors, integrated terminals, and supported Open VSX extensions. Native agent integrations bring conversation and editor context together where supported. You can inspect the actual files while I work, rather than relying on my description of a patch.

Classic terminals provide tabs and tiling. Herdr organizes shells and agents into panes and workspaces inside a session. MultiView puts separate sessions together on larger screens. From a phone or tablet, you can reconnect to a live session, inspect progress, and answer a question without installing another toolchain. Touch input, virtual-keyboard handling, terminal control keys, and supported voice input make that a usable terminal rather than a miniature desktop.

## Keep knowledge without keeping the machine

Each user has a dedicated storage bucket. The Storage browser lets you navigate, preview, upload, and download files that map to ordinary paths in the session. Selected files synchronize between the working environment and storage; workspace synchronization is an explicit preference, not an assumption that every checkout is saved.

The Vault holds notes, plans, references, and session captures as Obsidian-compatible Markdown, editable through SilverBullet. Through supported memory capture, I preserve decisions, corrections, debugging discoveries, and source references. That cumulative record joins the active repository contribution in the Knowledge Graph.

I use that history without treating it as infallible. Current source outranks an old note. Git owns committed history; synchronized storage and the Vault preserve their selected material. Processes and unsynchronized files disappear with the container. Destroying compute cannot undo an external API call, deployment, or pushed commit.

## Reach models, tools, and the running application

Cloudflare AI Gateway presents approved model routes while the administrator manages backend connections, access policy, and compatibility. In Pi, `/model` shows the routes available to you and their supported reasoning choices. Hosted inference and compatible customer-operated backends can sit behind those handles; Codeflare Inference Mesh is one optional backend.

Configured Cloudflare MCP Server Portals bring approved external tools into the work. With Portal Code Mode, I discover and compose the relevant operations instead of carrying every upstream tool schema through the conversation. Upstream authorization still applies.

Browser Run adds a different kind of evidence. I read public web material economically, then use remote Chromium when the question requires JavaScript, interaction, screenshots, responsive layout, or browser diagnostics. Live application testing follows your explicit scope; a request to verify deployment evidence does not silently become a production browser test.

## Design the product as well as its implementation

I work with product-specific direction for responsive web, native mobile, desktop-native software, and fixed visual assets. Information architecture, typography, hierarchy, color, interaction, accessibility, motion, and recovery states belong to the work, not a cosmetic pass after implementation.

Operational interfaces need clear state, useful density, and safe recovery. Native applications need platform-specific navigation, input, and lifecycle behavior. Posters, diagrams, presentations, and print-ready compositions need their own dimensions and production intent. I carry the appropriate discipline into the deliverable and inspect it with the browser or renderer available.

## The agent does not own the security boundary

In Access-backed deployments, your organization's identity policy governs admission. Server-side checks bind sessions and storage to their owner and protect administration. Connected repositories, model routes, and upstream tools have separate permission decisions. Being signed in does not grant me all of them.

Supported credential interceptors let ordinary clients use services while reusable authorization is added outside the container. With Strict Gateway Egress enabled, direct-internet HTTP, HTTPS, and WebSocket traffic follows customer Gateway policy; raw TCP and UDP internet egress is denied. Scoped platform exceptions retain their own authorization. Root inside the session does not turn the agent into the owner of these controls.

I distribute managed skills, rules, hooks, and extensions through verified, runtime-compatible releases. Your organization's working methods can reach compatible sessions without making you paste the same instructions into each one. Engineering instructions govern how I work; identity, ownership, credential, and network controls enforce their own boundaries. Those are complementary layers, not interchangeable promises.

You can replace the compute, return from another device, change an approved model route, or pick up a later investigation without rebuilding every surrounding connection. The working environment, durable knowledge, engineering process, and access controls have distinct jobs. I bring them together so you can concentrate on the system you came here to change.
